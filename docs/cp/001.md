### 1. Wing form factor

- Raymer's form factor is the highest: its term 1.34 M^0.18 is 1.18 at M 0.50. The other three have no Mach term or a weaker one.
- With fully turbulent flow, the form factor alone moves CD0 by 0.0011 (10.8 deg), 0.0009 (35 deg) and 0.0006 (47 deg).

### 2. Boundary-layer transition

- **Fixing transition.** The text says nothing about fixing transition: there is no roughness strip or grit in the sections on the model, the measurements or the corrections (PDF pages 6 and 7). The models were steel and supplied by an Air Force contractor. Natural transition is therefore inferred from the omission; the report does not state it.
- **Mixed flow.** Mixed flow uses the Prandtl-Schlichting transitional law, Cf = Cf_turbulent − 1700/Re, with the standard critical Reynolds number of 5 × 10^5. It is not adjusted.
- **Effect on the wing.** At M 0.50 the laminar run is the first 25 percent of the mean aerodynamic chord (Re 2.0 million). Wing Cf drops 22 percent, from 0.00385 to 0.00300.
- **Effect on the body.** Body Cf drops 8 percent (Re 6.4 million).
- **Net effect.** CD0 falls by 0.0018 to 0.0021, whatever the form factor.

- **Mean signed errors (7c).** Each variant's mean signed error is in the report, which does not pick one:

  - Raymer, mixed: −0.0000 / −0.0000 / +0.0005;

  - Choosing a variant would be calibration. Under the brief's rule it could only be chosen on the 35 deg wing and then validated on the other two.
- **Not captured: the sweep trend.** At M 0.50 the NACA curve falls by 0.0017 from 10.8 to 47 deg (0.01015 to 0.00848); the variants fall by 0.0001 to 0.0008. No variant gives that trend.

**Proposal: the model shows a band, not a line.**

- **Charts.** On the drag–Mach chart and on the polar, the model is a flat fill (accent at low opacity) between the lowest and the highest of the eight variants, with no centre line. Nothing selects one variant.
- **Legend.** "Model range: 4 textbook form factors × laminar run to Re 5×10^5 or fully turbulent".
- **Readout.** The readout gives the range, for example "CD0 0.0090 to 0.0118".
- **Above the model's limit.** The band stops where the model is outside its range, which is hatched, as now.
- **Scoring.** The error is the distance to the band, 0 inside. The band's spread is reported next to it.
- **Reliability strip.** A segment is "validated" only if the data lie in the band and the band's half-spread is within the precedent (0.0020). That stops a wide band from passing by width alone; here the half-spread is 0.0012 to 0.0017.
- **What the band does not cover.** The band carries only the parasite uncertainty. The transonic term is the same in every variant, so above Mcrit the band has no meaning of its own.

### 4. Korn-Mason on 4 percent wings

Report section 8, at CL = 0 with kA = 0.87:

| Wing | Mid-chord sweep | Korn-Mason Mdd | Drag rise, report text | Difference |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 10.8 deg | 0.0 deg | 0.830 | 0.92 | −0.090 |
| 35 deg | 27.0 deg | 0.926 | 0.951 (figure 9 samples; the text gives none) | −0.025 |
| 47 deg | 41.4 deg | 1.089 | 0.97 | +0.119 |

- **Sweep gain.** From 10.8 to 47 deg the measured drag-rise Mach number grows by 0.05; simple sweep theory gives 0.259, 5.2 times as much.
- **The error changes sign with sweep.** It is −0.09 at 10.8 deg and +0.12 at 47 deg. 35 deg falls where the two errors cross.
- **Mason's own notes** (Configuration Aerodynamics, transonic chapter):
  - Korn's relation is empirical, from 1970s transonic airfoil design.
  - Compared with older conventional airfoils at low thickness ratios, it is "overly pessimistic", which is the direction seen at 10.8 deg.
  - The strip method is validated on the Boeing 747-100 (kA 0.89) and 777 (kA 0.955). Mason says the results are sensitive to kA.
  - The chapter's wing section treats high-aspect-ratio transport wings, whose isobars unsweep at the root and tip.
- **Verdict.** None of these sources shows a validation at t/c = 0.04, at aspect ratio 3.5 or on a wing-body of this kind. The one documented comparison at low thickness errs in the direction we see. The method is outside its demonstrated range on these wings.
- **Lock's law.** It leaves the 10.8 deg wing without a valid prediction above M 0.88, so none of its nine figure 9 samples above M 0.90 is scored.

### 5. Raymer's transonic procedure (evaluated, not built)

Raymer's book was not available to check. The structure below comes from an open implementation that cites it: AeroSandbox, `aerosandbox.library.aerodynamics.transonic`, citing Raymer section 12.5.10.

- **How it works:**
  - The wave drag is anchored at M 1.2, from Sears-Haack, (D/q) = (9π/2)(Amax/l)², multiplied by an empirical wave-drag efficiency factor E_WD.
  - The value at M 1.05 is set equal to the M 1.2 value.
  - From Mcrit to Mdd the shape is Lock's law.
  - Between Mdd and M 1.05, and between 1.05 and 1.2, a curve is faired. AeroSandbox fixes the fairing with Hermite splines, which is the implementer's choice.
- **Against this dataset:**
  1. The M 1.2 anchor is outside the data, which end at M 1.12 to 1.13.
  2. The M 1.05 anchor falls in the report's gap (no data from M 1.04 to 1.08, dashed on figure 9).
  3. E_WD is a free factor; choosing it is calibration.
  4. The Mdd side still comes from Korn or from charts, so it does not fix check 4.
  5. The Sears-Haack formula assumes a closed body, but the tested body is a cylinder cut at the sting. The length to use is then a further choice.
- **What it would add, if built.** It is the right physics for the term that dominates here. On figure 9, CD0 rises by 0.0123 (10.8 deg), 0.0089 (35 deg) and 0.0077 (47 deg) from M 0.90 to its peak, against a subsonic CD0 of about 0.010.
- **Assessment.** Bringing Raymer's procedure forward on this dataset would mostly test our fairing and E_WD choices. If spike 2 is brought forward, the part worth taking is the one with no free factor: the wave drag of the configuration's area distribution, computed from the geometry and scored at M 1.10 to 1.13 and at the peak.
- **Reference to verify.** The same model family was tested at M 1.60 (report references 1 to 3, NACA RM L51K16a, L51K15a and L51K14a, 1952), which would cover a supersonic anchor. Whether they are the same models is not checked.

### 6. Verdict

- **Transonic.** The transport method, Korn-Mason with simple sweep and Lock's law, does not serve for these fighter wings in the transonic range. It is wrong in both directions as sweep changes; it overstates the gain from sweep by a factor of 5; and it has no valid output above M 0.88 on the least-swept wing. Moving kA would not fix the sign change with sweep.
- **Subsonic.** The CD0 discrepancy is not a failure of the transonic method. It comes from two parasite-drag choices the report does not support: Raymer's Mach term in the form factor and a fully turbulent boundary layer on a model with probably natural transition. Within those book choices the model spans the NACA curve, but with a spread of about 0.0030, so it cannot claim the precedent's ±0.0020 as a line.

### Decisions for the owner

1. CD0 output: band as proposed above, or a calibrated variant (chosen on 35 deg, validated on 10.8 and 47 deg), or keep the line and its band 4.
2. Download NACA RM L51I04a (body-alone data, and a 47 deg wing with several thickness ratios: a direct test of Korn's thickness term at fixed sweep) and RM L51K16a (the M 1.60 counterpart).
3. Tinling & Kolk: the brief's path when no error of ours is found. Two errors of ours are now candidates, so for CD0 it is no longer the first step. It would still show whether our implementation reproduces the precedent's 20 counts on the case where the precedent exists.
4. Transonic: report the method as not serving and keep it marked outside its range, or bring forward the area-distribution wave drag from spike 2.
